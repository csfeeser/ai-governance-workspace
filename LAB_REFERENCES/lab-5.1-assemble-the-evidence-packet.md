# Assemble the AI Evidence Packet

## Lab Objective

**Build a six-section evidence packet from a folder of candidate artifacts, identify what is missing, and record where each item is retained and for how long.**

This is the capstone. When an auditor asks you to show that oversight of an AI workflow actually happened, the answer is a packet: business purpose, system instructions and rules, sampled review records and scores, the override summary and reviewer audit, incident history, and the change-control log. In this lab you are given a folder with more artifacts than the packet needs, including a near-duplicate and one from a different workflow, plus a six-section packet form. You map what belongs, exclude what does not, name the gap, and complete the index.

In this lab, you will:
- Map candidate artifacts to the six packet sections
- Resolve a superseded near-duplicate by effective date
- Exclude an artifact that belongs to a different workflow, and say how you knew
- Identify a missing packet component and name who owns producing it
- Record a date, coverage period, and retention location for the packet

By the end of this lab you will be able to assemble an audit-ready evidence packet for any AI workflow.

## In Plain Words

- **What you are doing:** Imagine an auditor is coming, and they want proof that the company really kept an eye on PolicyPal. You have a folder of eight papers. You will choose the right ones, put each in the right place in a packet, and note anything that is missing.
- **Why it matters:** "We looked after it" is not proof. Papers are. A packet with the right papers in the right places is what lets a company pass an audit.
- **You do not have to:** download anything, open a spreadsheet, or type long answers. Each paper is a tab you can click, and nearly everything else is a drop-down menu.
- **If you feel lost:** every tab starts with a blue box that says what the tab is and what to do there. Read that box first.

**Words you will see in this lab**
- **Auditor:** a person whose job is to check that a company really did what it says it did.
- **Evidence packet:** a set of papers, organised into sections, that proves the company kept watch over its AI.
- **Coverage period:** the stretch of time a paper proves something about. For example, "2026-Q2" means April to June 2026.
- **Retention:** where the company keeps its records, and for how long. A record kept only on the AI platform can be deleted by the platform after about 180 days, so it must also be kept somewhere else.
- **Gap:** something that should be in the packet but is missing.
- **Superseded:** replaced by a newer version.

## Procedure

1. **See what is in the folder.** Your folder has eight papers, and each paper is a tab. The ninth tab, `My Packet`, is where you build the packet. There are more papers than you need, and choosing which ones belong is part of the job.
    - Look at the row of tabs across the top of the workspace.
    - The first eight are named `a1-business-purpose` up to `a8-finance-assistant-sample-q2`. Those are the papers.
    - The last one, `My Packet`, is your form.

0. **Read each paper enough to say what it is.** You cannot decide where a paper goes until you know what it is. There is nothing to type in this step.
    - Click each of the eight paper tabs, one after another.
    - Read the blue box at the top, and glance at the paper itself.
    - Try to say in one sentence what each paper is.

0. **See what the six sections of the packet are for.** An auditor wants six questions answered. The packet has one section for each question.
    - Click the `My Packet` tab and scroll through it.
    - Notice the six numbered sections.

    <details><summary>What is each section for? Click here.</summary>

    1. **Business purpose statement:** what is this tool for?
    2. **System instructions and baseline business rules:** what was it told to do?
    3. **Sampled review records and quality scores:** did you check its quality, and when?
    4. **HITL override summary and reviewer quality audit:** was the human checking real? ("HITL" means "human in the loop", which is a person checking the AI.)
    5. **Incident and escalation history:** what went wrong, and what did you do about it?
    6. **Change-control and approval log:** what changed, and who approved it?

    </details>

0. **Place the two papers that are easy to place.** Two of the papers clearly answer one question each. Start with the easy ones to build momentum.
    - On the `My Packet` tab, find the section **1. Business purpose statement**.
    - Use the **File** drop-down to pick the paper that says what PolicyPal is for.
    - Then find the section **5. Incident and escalation history** and pick the paper that lists past problems.

    <details><summary>ANSWER:</summary>

    Section 1 is `a1-business-purpose`. Section 5 is `a7-incident-history`.

    </details>

0. **Choose between the two sets of rules.** The folder has two versions of PolicyPal's rules. The packet needs the one that was in force, and the older one should be left out. The way to tell is the date at the top of each paper.
    - Click the `a2-system-instructions-v3` tab and note the date it started.
    - Click the `a3-system-instructions-v2` tab and note the date it started.
    - Click the `My Packet` tab. In the section **2. System instructions and baseline business rules**, pick the **File** that is the newer version.
    - In the same section, use the drop-down under the question about `a3` to say why that paper is left out.

    <details><summary>ANSWER:</summary>

    `a2-system-instructions-v3` started on 2026-06-01, so it is the current version. `a3-system-instructions-v2` started on 2026-03-02 and has been replaced. It is left out because it is an older version of a paper that is already in.

    </details>

0. **Spot the paper about a different AI tool.** Two of the papers are tables of marked answers. Only one is about PolicyPal. A packet about PolicyPal should only hold papers about PolicyPal.
    - Click the `a4-review-sample-q2` tab and read the first column, `workflow`.
    - Click the `a8-finance-assistant-sample-q2` tab and read its first column, `workflow`.
    - Look at the questions too. They are about different things.
    - On the `My Packet` tab, in the section **3. Sampled review records and quality scores**, pick the paper about PolicyPal.
    - In the same section, use the drop-down under the question about `a8` to say why that paper is left out.

    <details><summary>Hint 1: where to look</summary>

    Compare the `workflow` column on the two tabs. Then compare the questions, and how many scoring columns each has.

    </details>

    <details><summary>ANSWER:</summary>

    `a4-review-sample-q2` is the PolicyPal one. `a8-finance-assistant-sample-q2` is about a different AI tool, the finance assistant. Its `workflow` column, its questions, and its rulebook are all different. It is left out because it belongs to a different workflow.

    </details>

0. **Place the two papers for section 4.** Section 4 is about whether the human checking was real, and it has two slots: the summary of the checking, and the plan to fix a checker who was not doing it properly.
    - On the `My Packet` tab, find the two sections named **4. HITL override summary and reviewer quality audit**. One says (first item) and one says (second item).
    - Use the **File** drop-down in each one to pick a paper.

    <details><summary>ANSWER:</summary>

    `a5-hitl-override-summary-q2` and `a6-remediation-plan-RA`.

    </details>

0. **Check whether anything is missing.** An auditor will look for all six sections. Section 6 needs a paper that records what changed and who approved it. You need to check whether you have one.
    - Click through the eight paper tabs again and ask: is any of these a record of changes and approvals?
    - On the `My Packet` tab, in the section **6. Change-control and approval log**, use the drop-down to record what you found.

    <details><summary>ANSWER:</summary>

    It is not there. Pick `None in the folder`.

    </details>

0. **Write down the gap.** A missing paper is bad. A missing paper that nobody mentioned is worse, because the auditor will find it and ask why you hid it. So you write it down, and say who will fix it and by when.
    - On the `My Packet` tab, find the section **Known gaps**.
    - Write what is missing, who is responsible for producing it, and by when.

    <details><summary>Hint 1: a sentence to start from</summary>

    Fill in the blanks: "Section ______ (the ______) is missing. Owner: the function that maintains ______. Due ______."

    </details>

    <details><summary>Hint 2: an example answer</summary>

    "Section 6 (change-control and approval log) is missing. Owner: the function that maintains the workflow's configuration records. Due before this packet is submitted."

    </details>

0. **Give each paper its date and time span.** An auditor wants to know when each paper was made and what stretch of time it proves. Every paper shows this near the top.
    - For each paper you chose, find its date and the period it covers. Look near the top of its tab.
    - On the `My Packet` tab, use the box called **Date and coverage period** in each section to type both.

    <details><summary>Hint 1: a sentence to start from</summary>

    Fill in the blanks: "Dated ______; covers ______."

    </details>

    <details><summary>Hint 2: an example answer</summary>

    For the system instructions: "Dated 2026-06-01; covers 2026-06-01 onward."

    </details>

0. **Say where the copies are kept.** Records that only exist inside the AI platform are deleted by the platform after about 180 days. An auditor may come after that, so copies must be kept somewhere safe that belongs to the company.
    - Near the bottom of the `My Packet` tab, find the section **Where the exported copies are kept**.
    - Use the drop-down to pick the right place.

    <details><summary>ANSWER:</summary>

    `GRC evidence library / PolicyPal / 2026`. That is a governance system that belongs to the company. "Copilot audit log", "Purview" and "Claude workspace" are the platforms themselves, and the evidence in them would be gone in about seven months.

    </details>

0. **Fill in the top of the packet.** The header says what the packet is about and who made it.
    - Find the section **Packet header** at the top of the `My Packet` tab.
    - Type the workflow (the name of the AI tool), the time span the packet covers, and who made it and when.

    <details><summary>Hint 1: how to work out the time span</summary>

    The packet's time span is the stretch of time the papers cover. Look at the dates and periods you wrote in the last steps.

    </details>

0. **Look at the packet as an auditor would.** You have built the packet. Now check whether it really shows that PolicyPal was looked after for the whole time.
    - Scroll back through the whole `My Packet` tab.
    - Imagine you are an auditor who has never seen this before.
    - Ask: does this prove that oversight really happened for the whole period?
    - Find the section that looks weakest, and improve it by typing in that box.

0. **Check that you have finished.** The workspace keeps count of the boxes you have filled in.
    - Look at the top right of the workspace. It says something like `X of Y answers filled in`.
    - When X is the same as Y, you are done.
    - Your answers save automatically as you type, so there is nothing to save or send.
    - If you would like a copy for yourself, click the `Save as PDF` button on the `My Packet` tab.
